# ICOR for Life - INKLINE

**A vault that reads like pages, not like an interface.**

Two rooms: an ink room for the dark, a paper room for the light. One marker
orange for the few things that need your attention. Your highlights and your
comments render the way they would in a well-marked book.

![INKLINE screenshot](screenshot.png)

Part of the [ICOR for Life](https://myicor.com) suite.

## What it is for

Most themes add colour. This one takes it away.

When everything is coloured, nothing stands out, and a vault you read for
hours becomes a vault you squint at. Almost everything here is ink on paper.
The one orange is reserved, so when you see it, it means something.

## What you get

**Two full modes**, ink and paper, designed as one system rather than one
inverted into the other.

**A handwritten layer.** Blockquotes, note, tip and quote callouts and your
`%%comments%%` render in a handwriting face, like a teacher writing in the
margin. `==Highlights==` are a marker wash, no glow.

**Mermaid diagrams that survive dark mode.** Obsidian's own dark mode inverts
the whole diagram, which turns a careful drawing into a photo negative. This
colours them properly in both rooms, with your reading typeface inside the
nodes.

**Room icons for numbered folders.** A root folder named `00` through `07`
drops its number in the sidebar and gains an inked icon in its own hue. Built
for the ICOR for Life vault; harmless in any other, where it simply matches
nothing.

**Fonts that make no requests.** Four typefaces ship inside the theme itself.
It connects to nothing.

## Getting started

Requires Obsidian 1.5.8 or newer.

**Settings, Appearance, Themes, Manage**, search "ICOR for Life - INKLINE",
install, use.

Everything is on out of the box.

## Changing it

Install the [Style Settings](https://obsidian.md/plugins?id=obsidian-style-settings)
plugin and the theme appears there with five switches: turn off the
handwritten layer, hide the banner, turn off room icons and colours, reduce
Obsidian's own controls, and hide the left ribbon.

The last two are off by default because they take a control away from
Obsidian, and a theme should not do that to a vault it was just installed
into.

The plugin is optional. The theme is complete without it.

## What it touches

- **Nothing.** It is a theme. It reads no notes, writes no files and makes no
  network request of any kind.

## Good to know

- **Beta.** In daily use in a real vault, and you will find rough edges. If
  something looks off, open an issue.
